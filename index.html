Field: economic models and measurement. I am interested in the theory of the empirical methods applied microeconomists use — identification, robustness, and diagnostics for demand, production, and the measurement of market power — so that the estimates carrying a counterfactual into a public decision can be trusted.

So far I have written one paper — my master’s thesis, on how market power interacts with public policy. It estimated markups for a panel of Czech construction firms, public suppliers and private alike, and measured how a firm’s markup changes as it enters and leaves public procurement, combining production-function estimation with causal-inference methods on administrative microdata. It received the Czech Economic Society’s Young Economist of the Year Award (first place, 2025), and I have kept revising it during my Yale coursework.

At Yale I work as a research assistant to Professors Seth Zimmerman and Barbara Biasi and take Ph.D. courses in empirical industrial organization and econometrics as a visiting student. Before coming to New Haven, I earned an M.Sc. in Economics from the Stockholm School of Economics and a B.Sc. in Economic Theory from Charles University, and held research assistant positions at the Institute for International Economic Studies (IIES), Stockholm University, and at CERGE-EI in Prague.
