Graduate coursework and the work that came out of it. Links point to my own code and writing; instructors' materials are not reposted. The complete course list, with grades, is in my CV.

Yale University — Ph.D. coursework (2025–present)

  • Empirical industrial organization — three replication-grade implementations from Industrial Organization I (ECON 600, Honors): BLP demand estimation and merger simulation with pyBLP (random-coefficients logit, optimal instruments, diversion ratios); Rust (1987) dynamic programming; dynamic entry/exit games (Bajari–Benkard–Levin forward simulation).
  • Econometrics of Dependent Data / Econometrics IV (ECON 5553, Prof. Tim Christensen, Honors) — panel data, time series, misspecification-robust inference. My oral exam was a presentation of Andrews, Barahona, Gentzkow, Rambachan & Shapiro (QJE 2025), which I developed into a research proposal on strong exclusion and applied in my own paper. Also a referee report on Borusyak, Chen, Hull & Lei, Nonparametric Identification of Demand without Exogenous Product Characteristics (NBER WP 34842), and problem-set write-ups.
  • Industrial Organization II (ECON 601, audited) — auctions, entry, dynamic games. Wrote a structural research proposal on entry barriers and markup premiums in procurement, refereed a paper on political connections in Czech procurement, and presented my working paper in class.
  • Econometrics III (ECON 5552, Prof. Yuichi Kitamura; audited, problem sets completed) — nonparametric identification and inference; problem-set write-ups.
  • Measure Theory and Integration (MATH 5200, Fall 2026, for credit); planned Spring 2027 — Functional Analysis and Econometrics V (audit).

Stockholm University — doctoral econometrics (2024, concurrent with the M.S.)

  • Econometrics I (EC38002; Profs. Mitchell Downey & Markus Jäntti) and Econometrics II (EC38003; Profs. Konrad Burchardi & David Schönholzer) — the first-year Ph.D. sequence; Econometrics I taught from Bruce Hansen's Probability and Statistics for Economists. Problem sets solved in R and Python: econometrics (Metrics1SU, Metrics2SU). Applied Empirical Economics I covered research design and applied methods.

Stockholm School of Economics — M.S. Economics (2023–25)

  • Applied econometrics reports (OLS, IV, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity), a demand-estimation project in IO, and a time-series sequence (ARMA, unit roots, cointegration, VAR). Reports and code: econometrics (MScSSE).
  • Proof-based advanced microeconomic theory and advanced mathematics; computational macroeconomics following the QuantEcon Julia lectures.
  • Econometric Game 2024 (University of Amsterdam) — SSE team captain; case study on causal effects in education using PISA data and a school-starting-age regression discontinuity design.
